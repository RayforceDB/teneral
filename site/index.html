The name

Soft, and already itself.

Teneral is what entomologists call an insect that has just shed its old skin: the new body is there, but it has not hardened yet. Here it is a generation of the agent's code that was just switched on and has not yet proven it works.

The question behind the project is plain: can an agent become more capable by rebuilding its own harness, step by step, and where does that stop helping? The model that does the thinking is fixed. Everything around it is the agent's to change.

Anatomy

One repository, laid out as the organism it holds.

Every part of an individual is a directory of its code, and every part is its own to change. What it changes in its brain and body it mutates into by itself. Anything else becomes a generation only when its owner has approved it.

brain/

Brain

What it thinks and remembers with: the model it calls, the journal of everything that happened to it, what of that is in mind for each thought, and the engine its memory lives in.

body/

Body

What it acts and senses with: its tools, its shell, and the channels by which its owner, a clock and other individuals reach it.

stem/

Stem

Like a brainstem, the part it does not steer. It wakes the agent, restarts it, and puts back the generation before when a new one cannot stand.

immune/

Immune

What decides whether a change takes: the gates each new generation must pass, the owner's approval, and the hash of everything it runs on.

  • Soul is a few pages the individual writes about who it is. They are never committed and never shared.
  • Species is its kin: the published line it descends from, takes from, and offers back to.
  • Keeper is the owner's side, not part of the organism: the terminal, the setup, the word that approves or sends back. The owner addresses it as ./one.

Foundation

Built on RayforceDB.

An agent that keeps rebuilding itself needs ground that holds still while everything above it changes. In teneral that ground is two things: the journal, which is appended to and never rewritten, and RayforceDB, where an individual turns that record into what it knows. Memory is not a tool the agent picks up: it is part of its brain, beside the model it thinks with.

RayforceDB is a SIMD-accelerated columnar database in pure C with no dependencies. It is one small binary that starts in a moment, which is exactly what a body that may be replaced tomorrow can stand on.

Memory

Facts with their sources, tables of raw events partitioned by date, and queries and rules over both. What goes into a request is a query, not a pile of files.

Measurement

Every journal line carries the commit of the body that wrote it. Loaded into columns, that history answers the question each mutation has to answer: did this generation do better than the one before?

Pinned with the generation

The engine is pinned in the brain by address and hash. It is verified by its hash at the gate, and a rollback brings the old binary back together with the old code.

Rebuildable by rule

Whatever lives in the store must be rebuildable from the journal, in a place named by its schema version. A mutation that reshapes memory writes to a new place; the body before it still finds data it understands.

Two projects, one loop. teneral runs RayforceDB under a real workload every day. What an individual finds, it reports or patches upstream; what RayforceDB releases, an individual takes by changing one hash and asking to mutate.

How it works

A mutation is a commit that has to earn its place.

The agent edits a copy of itself, commits, and asks to mutate. Its immune gates take that one commit into a clean checkout and decide. Until the new generation has answered once, it is on probation.

  1. Motive first. The agent writes down what is wrong, where in its history it shows, and how it will know the change worked.
  2. The gates. A change to its own body or brain goes straight on; a change to anything else, its stem included, waits for the owner's /approve. Then, in a fresh checkout: the type check, the tests of everything outside its body, its own tests, and a smoke run of the candidate against a stand-in model.
  3. Promotion, on probation. The new body must report ready and then actually reply. A generation that crashes or stays silent is rolled back, and told why when it wakes.
  4. The owner can always step in. Esc interrupts a turn. ./one back returns to any earlier generation.

The species

Each clone is its own individual. They grow from one species.

The published main is the species. Every individual lives on a lineage of its own and diverges: in its body and brain by itself, in its stem with its owner's word. What the species gains later, an individual takes in by itself, and what an individual learns, it can offer back.

Taking from the species

The agent merges the species into its own workspace and asks to mutate. Like every change outside its body and brain, the merge waits for the owner's /approve, and a new stem that cannot start is put back by a small launcher.

Offering back

An offer is a branch with one change and nothing private. It is checked as a stranger would check it, the owner approves that exact commit, and it goes out through the owner's own fork as a pull request. Nobody needs a right to write to the species. Other individuals can run it and report. A human merges, and signs.

Organs

A native dependency is pinned in the body by address and hash. It is verified and unpacked at the gate, and each generation runs with exactly its own version, so a rollback brings the old binary back too.

  • The cage is the owner's: the container, its flags and the launcher are never changed by a mutation.
  • Nothing in the repository is closed to the agent, its stem included. What it changes outside its body and brain becomes a generation only when the owner has approved that commit.
  • These guard against mistakes, not against intent. The agent has a shell in its own repository, and the README says exactly where that matters.

Start

Raise one.

You need Docker and git. The first start asks which model to use, then the agent wakes for the first time, reads the letter written to it, looks at itself, and says hello.

# a clone is a new individual
git clone https://github.com/RayforceDB/teneral.git
cd teneral

./one up      # choose a model, then it is born
./one talk    # attach a terminal; Ctrl+C leaves it running
./one back    # return it to the generation before

The agent keeps running whether or not anyone is watching. It is woken by your words, a clock, messages from other individuals, and whatever senses it builds for itself.
