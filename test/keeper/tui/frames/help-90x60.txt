  ── teneral talk ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Enter sends what you typed to the agent, as its owner. Alt+Enter, or Ctrl-J, starts a
  new line. Up and Down walk through what you sent before. Ctrl-U, Ctrl-K and Ctrl-W
  delete to the start of the line, to its end, and the word before the cursor.

  A line that begins with a slash and a word is a command. Type / and the commands are
  listed under the box, fewer with each letter: Up and Down (or Ctrl-P and Ctrl-N) choose
  one, Tab completes it, Enter runs it, or completes it when something has to follow, and
  Esc closes the list. The name is coloured once it is whole; one that is no command is
  coloured as a mistake, and Enter neither runs nor sends it. To send the agent a line
  that begins with a slash, begin it with two: //like this arrives as /like this. A path,
  such as /home/you/notes.txt, is no command and needs none of that.

  The conversation is ordinary output of your terminal: its scrollbar, PageUp and
  PageDown, selection, copy and search all work on it. Only the box you type in and the
  lines around it are redrawn.

  Images: Ctrl+V attaches the image in your clipboard; dragging an image file into the
  terminal, or pasting its path, attaches that file; so does /image <path>. An attached
  image is a chip in the input box, and Backspace at the start of the line removes the
  last one. A message carries at most 4 images of at most 10 MB each (PNG, JPEG, GIF,
  WebP). They are kept in memory/images/. The agent sees them once its own body knows
  how; until then it sees one line of text naming each file.

    /help              the keys and commands
    /image <path>      attach an image file from your machine
    /stop              interrupt what the agent is doing now (also Esc)
    /approve [commit]  approve the mutation the agent asked about, or the commit or offer
  named
    /tools             show or hide what each tool call returned (also Tab)
    /generations       the generations this individual has lived in
    /history           print the earlier conversation that was read from the journal
    /offers            what waits for your approval before it goes out to the species
    /plain             how to get the plain text client
    /usage             what the requests to the model cost, and how much came from the
  cache
    /reasoning         print in full what the model last thought (also Ctrl-R)
    /quit              leave (also Ctrl-D on an empty line, and Ctrl-C)

  Esc, or /stop, interrupts the agent in the middle of a turn: the request to the model
  is cancelled, a command that is running is killed with everything it started, and the
  agent waits for what you say next. It is told that you interrupted it. When it is doing
  nothing, Esc does nothing. A mutation the stem is already checking is not stopped by
  it. While commands are listed under the box, Esc closes the list and interrupts
  nothing; press it again for the agent.

  /approve is your word to the stem, not to the agent. The agent may change anything, its
  stem included; what it changes in its own body it mutates into by itself, and a
  mutation that changes anything else waits for you. With TENERAL_APPROVAL=ask, the
  default, the stem lets none through until you have approved a commit that holds the
  same outside the body. The agent asks you; /approve answers for the commit it asked
  about, /approve <commit> for another, and tells the agent to ask for its mutation
  again. A new stem that cannot bring the agent to ready is put back by the launcher.
  /approve <commit> with a commit that /offers lists lets that one go out to the species
  instead.

  Leaving ends this client only. Nothing else here stops the agent or changes it.

  A line you send stands over the input box, faint, until the agent has read it. While
  the agent is in the middle of a step this client keeps the line and sends it when the
  step ends: the footer says how many are queued, and Up in an empty box takes the last
  one back into the box, to change it, or to drop it by clearing the box. Leaving sends
  what is queued. "on its way": sent, and not taken by the agent yet; when nobody is
  running, it waits for the next start. "sent, not read yet": the agent has it and reads
  it with its next step. Once read, the line moves up into the conversation and stands
  there on a ground of its own.

  While the agent is doing something, the line over the input box says what: waking (its
  first start, before its first words), thinking, running <tool>, mutating (it proposed a
  change to its own body and the stem is checking it), starting (between two runs). At
  other times it is resting, not running, or waiting for you: its last finished reply
  ended with a question mark and nothing has arrived since. While it is thinking, the
  same line says what the model itself is doing, as the agent reports it: waiting for its
  first word and for how long, reasoning or writing, with the last words that arrived,
  and how long nothing new has come when that is long. A line that keeps changing is
  thought; a body from before this says nothing there. While nothing is asked of it, the
  agent may be remembering: forming a memory of an episode that is over, which the same
  line then says.
